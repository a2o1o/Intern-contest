The task

Compose the next WhatsApp message from four context layers.

What candidates build

A deterministic compose(category, merchant, trigger, customer?) function that returns the next message, CTA, send identity, suppression key, and rationale.

What Vera is

magicpin's merchant assistant for WhatsApp. It helps businesses improve Google profiles, launch campaigns, answer customer questions, and act on growth opportunities.

What wins

High-specificity, category-correct, trigger-aware messages that make a merchant think: "this is about my business, and replying is low effort."

How scoring works

Judges reward decision quality, not just fluent copy.

Each submission is scored across five 0-10 dimensions. The best bots choose what matters, ignore weak facts, avoid hallucination, and turn context into a reply-worthy message.

10

Decision quality

Can the bot choose the best context signal for the moment? A strong answer uses the trigger, merchant state, and category constraints to decide the next move.

10

Specificity

Concrete numbers, dates, local context, source citations, and real offers from the input.

10

Category fit

Dentists sound clinical. Salons sound visual and practical. Restaurants sound timely and local.

10

Merchant fit

Personalizes to the merchant's metrics, language, offer catalog, and past conversation behavior.

10

Engagement compulsion

Creates a clear reason to reply now using curiosity, loss aversion, proof, or low-effort action.

1. Decision quality

Decision quality is the difference between copying all available data and choosing the one fact that should drive the message. If Dr. Meera has low CTR and a new dental digest item, the bot should decide whether the strongest move is research value, performance recovery, or patient education.

2. Engagement

Engagement means the merchant is likely to respond, not just understand. Winning messages are short, specific, and easy to answer. They often end with one low-friction ask: "Want me to draft it?" or "Reply YES and I will set it up."

3. Bold high-compulsion messaging

Bold does not mean hype. It means a sharp hook: missed demand, a local peer benchmark, a useful new insight, or a ready-made action. The message should create urgency without inventing facts.

4. Generic messages lose

"Boost your business with 20% off" could be sent to anyone. The judge penalizes that. "Your CTR is 2.1% vs 3.0% South Delhi peer median. Want me to test Dental Cleaning @ Rs 299?" is grounded.

Message craft

What good looks like.

Generic

Hi Doctor, we can help you increase sales with a special discount campaign. Would you like to run an offer today?

  • No trigger
  • No merchant fact
  • No category voice
High compulsion

Dr. Meera, your CTR is 2.1% vs 3.0% South Delhi peer median. You already have Dental Cleaning @ Rs 299. Want me to draft a 160-char patient WhatsApp around it?

  • Specific benchmark
  • Real offer
  • Single CTA

Compulsion levers

Use verifiable facts, loss aversion, social proof, curiosity, reciprocity, effort externalization, and a single binary commitment.

Constraints

Respect the 24h WhatsApp session window, 320-character body cap, one primary CTA, no URLs, and no fabricated claims.

Twist

After submission, judges inject fresh digest items, updated merchant performance, new triggers, and customer contexts to test adaptability.

How the live test works

Candidates expose a public HTTP bot. The judge drives the whole conversation.

During testing, magicpin's harness pushes context, wakes the bot on simulated time ticks, plays merchant replies, and scores every message. The bot must stay stateful, fast, and grounded in the contexts it has received.

POST

/v1/context

Receives category, merchant, customer, and trigger updates. Idempotent on scope, context ID, and version.

POST

/v1/tick

Periodic wake-up. Bot decides whether to proactively send zero or more messages for active triggers.

POST

/v1/reply

Judge plays the merchant or customer. Bot must answer with send, wait, or end within 10 seconds.

GET

/v1/healthz

Liveness probe with loaded-context counts. Three consecutive failures disqualify the bot for the slot.

GET

/v1/metadata

Team identity, members, model, approach, contact email, version, and submitted timestamp.

01

Warmup

Health check, metadata check, then 255 base contexts: 5 categories, 50 merchants, 200 customers, 0 triggers.

02

Test window

60 simulated minutes. Every 5 simulated minutes, the judge pushes updates and calls /v1/tick.

03

Adaptive injection

Fresh digest items, performance shifts, new triggers, and surprise customer scopes arrive mid-test.

04

Replay test

Top 10 face auto-reply hell, intent transition, and hostile/off-topic merchant scenarios.

05

Score report

Teams receive per-message scores, context logs, transcripts, timeline, and judge rationale.

AuthBearer team token on every /v1 request
Timeout10 seconds per call
Rate10 requests/sec from judge
Payload500 KB context cap
Tick cap20 actions per tick

Fully automated selection

How 1,000 participants become the top 10 without human review.

The selection process is staged. Cheap contract checks run first, then structured message scoring, then adaptive tests, then deep replay. Every decision is logged with evidence so the final ranking is auditable instead of subjective.

1,000

Submission gate

Reject unreachable bots, missing endpoints, failed auth, invalid metadata, malformed JSON, or warmup failure.

~700

Contract test

Run the local simulator scenarios for schema compliance, latency, context persistence, and response validity.

250

Canonical test

Score 30 fixed trigger cases using the same rubric for every participant. Keep the strongest message composers.

75

Adaptive test

Inject fresh digest, performance, trigger, and customer context. Reward bots that update behavior without hallucinating.

25

Replay test

Run multi-turn merchant simulations: auto-reply hell, intent transition, hostile/off-topic, and customer recall.

10

Robustness final

Repeat randomized equivalent scenarios, add timeout pressure and restraint cases, then rank by aggregate score.

Final score formula

45% message quality
 20% adaptive context use
 20% multi-turn handling
 10% operational reliability
  5% restraint / anti-spam
- penalties

Hard penalties

  • Hallucinated fact or fake citation
  • URL in message body
  • Body over 320 characters
  • Missing auth or repeated health failures
  • Repeated identical body in one conversation
  • Ignoring not-interested or explicit yes intent

Judge design principle

The LLM judge does not directly “pick winners.” It scores each message and conversation against structured dimensions with cited evidence. Ranking happens numerically from those logged scores.
